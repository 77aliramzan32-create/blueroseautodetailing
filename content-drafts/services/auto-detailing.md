### What a Full Detail Actually Includes

Auto detailing and a car wash are not the same job. A tunnel wash takes four minutes and moves dirt from the surface. A full detail takes most of a day and addresses what the wash leaves behind.

**Exterior:** The paint starts with a pre-rinse and iron fallout remover — a product that turns purple on contact as it dissolves brake dust and ferrous rail particles that have bonded into the clear coat. Wash alone can't remove these. After the chemical step, a clay bar pass lifts anything that's mechanically embedded: tree sap residue, industrial overspray, pollen that's been heat-bonded through a summer. The actual wash uses two buckets with grit guards to prevent cross-contamination — the reason single-bucket washing creates swirl marks. A machine polish follows to remove light oxidation and surface haze, then a paint sealant adds 3–6 months of protection.

**Interior:** Vacuuming reaches every crevice — under the seats, the seat rail channels, the floor vents. Steam cleaning follows: it kills bacteria, loosens ground-in soil in fabric and carpet, and cleans vents and headliner without the harsh chemicals that degrade plastic trim or stain leather stitching. Carpet extraction pulls embedded grit and staining that vacuuming misses. Leather gets cleaned first, then conditioned — conditioning alone on dirty leather seals contamination in. Glass is cleaned streak-free on both sides. Dashboard and trim are dressed with a matte, UV-stable, non-slip product. Gloss dressings on dashboards create windshield glare; shine spray on steering wheels and pedals is a safety hazard. We don't use them.

### Is a Full Detail Right for Your Situation?

**Almost always yes — if any of these apply:**
- Your paint feels rough or looks hazy after washing — bonded contamination that a wash won't remove
- Your interior has accumulated pet hair, food debris, carpet staining, or a general buildup from months of use
- You're selling the car — a detail before listing improves photos, removes smell, and supports a stronger asking price
- You're preparing for a ceramic coating — decontamination is the required first step before any coating
- You just bought a used vehicle and don't know what the previous owner left behind

**Not what you need if:**
- Your paint has visible swirl marks and spider webbing that you want actually removed — a detail's machine polish is maintenance-level gloss enhancement, not correction. That's [paint correction](/services/paint-correction).
- You have smoke odor that's been embedded for years — a single detail can reduce it, but we'll tell you before we start whether ozone treatment is the right call. We won't take money for a service that won't solve the problem.
- Your leather is cracking through the finish — conditioning can slow progression, but cracked leather needs reconditioning products or panel replacement, not a standard detail. We'll tell you which applies at the assessment.

### What Happens in the Bay

**Step 1 — Pre-Rinse and Wheel Clean**
Wheels and wheel wells go first — they carry the heaviest brake dust and road grime. Cleaning them last cross-contaminates everything already washed. High-pressure rinse removes loose surface debris before any contact wash begins.

**Step 2 — Iron Fallout Removal**
Iron fallout remover is applied to all painted surfaces. It reacts with the ferrous particles — brake dust, rail dust — bonded into the clear coat, turning purple on contact. This step removes contamination that no amount of washing will lift. It's one of the most visible demonstrations of what a detail does that a car wash doesn't.

**Step 3 — Clay Bar Decontamination**
After the chemical step, a clay bar pass removes anything that's mechanically embedded in the surface. Tree sap residue, industrial overspray, pollen that's heat-bonded over a summer. After clay, paint should feel like glass. If it still feels rough, the clay goes back over it.

**Step 4 — Two-Bucket Hand Wash**
pH-neutral shampoo, two buckets with grit guards. One bucket for the wash mitt, one for rinsing. This prevents the grit that caused the swirl marks in the first place from going back on the paint. The mitt is wrung out in the rinse bucket before going back to the wash bucket on every panel.

**Step 5 — Interior Deep Clean**
Vacuum of all surfaces including crevices, seat rails, and vents. Steam clean of fabric surfaces, vents, headliner, and plastic trim. Carpet extraction with hot water to pull embedded soiling and staining. Leather cleaned with a pH-balanced cleaner, then conditioned. Glass cleaned streak-free on both sides. Trim dressed with a matte, UV-stable product.

**Step 6 — Machine Polish, Sealant, and Inspection**
A light machine polish removes minor surface oxidation and water spots, and brings back the gloss the paint is capable of. A paint sealant over the corrected surface adds 3–6 months of protection. Tristan inspects the vehicle under the LED bay before we call it done — exterior panel by panel, interior for any missed areas, glass for streaks. That inspection is the last step before the keys go back to you.

### What to Expect

**What a full detail does:**

After the exterior work, paint should feel smooth to the touch and show noticeably more depth and gloss than before. The clay bar step removes the contamination layer that makes washed paint look dull even when it's clean. The sealant gives the surface hydrophobic properties for the next few months — water will bead and run off rather than sitting and leaving mineral deposits.

After the interior work, surfaces should be free of embedded soiling, odors should be significantly reduced, and leather should feel clean and supple rather than dry. Carpet extraction goes deep enough that light staining from food and liquid typically clears completely. Pet hair is removed systematically from fabric surfaces.

**What a detail doesn't do:**

A standard detail won't remove swirl marks and deep scratches — those are in the clear coat itself and require machine correction to fix. A detail polish improves gloss; it doesn't level the surface. If you want scratches removed, the starting point is [paint correction](/services/paint-correction).

Old set stains — coffee, pet accidents, red wine that dried months ago — may not fully remove with extraction alone. We'll assess at intake and tell you before starting whether the staining will respond to extraction or whether it needs a more targeted treatment.

Embedded smoke odor from years of in-car smoking doesn't come out with extraction. The odor compounds have penetrated every porous surface. Ozone treatment is the appropriate fix; we'll tell you that up front rather than charge for extraction that won't solve it.

**How long the results last:**

The paint sealant holds for 3–6 months under normal driving conditions. After that, it needs reapplication or an upgrade to ceramic coating for longer-term protection. The interior stays clean in proportion to how the car is used — a daily driver with kids will need more frequent attention than a weekend car. Most customers on a maintenance schedule come back every 6–12 months; higher-use vehicles every 3–4 months.

### What We See on Springfield & Eugene Cars

Spring in the Willamette Valley is the busiest detail season for a reason. The grass seed fields surrounding Eugene and Springfield — Lane County is one of the largest grass seed-producing regions in the country — release pollen through April and May that settles on wet paint and bonds to the clear coat surface. A standard car wash rinses the loose layer; the clay bar step in a full detail removes what's bonded in. By late May, most vehicles that park outside have a full season of accumulated pollen that's contributing to paint haze even after washing.

The other pattern we see consistently: outdoor lifestyle vehicles that take real abuse inside. Subarus and Tacomas that carry dogs, camping gear, and muddy gear from the Cascades or the coast. The interior condition on these vehicles often surprises owners who've been living with it gradually. Extraction, steam, and leather conditioning reset them to a baseline that's maintainable — and once the car is clean, the maintenance detail is a fraction of the work.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**How much does auto detailing cost?**
[NEEDS FROM TRISTAN: Starting prices by vehicle size]. Price depends on vehicle size, interior condition, and which services are requested. We quote every job individually before starting. Call (541) 337-9893 to describe your vehicle and we'll give you a range before you commit.

**How long does a full detail take?**
A full interior and exterior detail typically takes 4–8 hours depending on vehicle size and condition. A compact car in reasonable condition is usually 4–5 hours; a large SUV or a vehicle with heavy interior soiling can be a full day. We'll give you a realistic estimate when you call or book online.

**What's the difference between a car wash and auto detailing?**
A tunnel car wash takes 4 minutes and removes surface dirt. A full detail takes most of a day and addresses what the wash leaves behind: bonded contamination removed from the paint with iron fallout remover and clay bar, carpet extracted rather than vacuumed over, leather cleaned and conditioned, and a machine polish and sealant applied to the paint. They're not comparable services.

**Can detailing remove pet hair and pet odor?**
Pet hair: yes, systematically — from fabric seats, carpet, and crevices. Pet odor: depends on severity. A fresh or mild pet smell typically clears with extraction and steam treatment. A strong, embedded odor from years of multiple pets may require an enzyme treatment or ozone treatment to fully neutralize rather than mask. We assess at intake and tell you which approach applies before we quote the job.

**Do I need to empty my car before bringing it in?**
Remove personal items, paperwork, and anything you'd rather keep safe. You don't need to vacuum or pre-clean — that's our job. Leave the car in the condition it's in. It helps us see the actual work scope when we quote the job.

**How often should I get my car detailed?**
For most daily drivers in Springfield and Eugene: a full detail once or twice a year keeps the vehicle in consistently good condition. High-use vehicles with kids, pets, or regular outdoor use benefit from interior maintenance every 3–4 months. If you have a ceramic coating, the paint stays cleaner longer and the maintenance schedule is lighter — contamination doesn't bond as readily to the coated surface.
